Chapter one

You already know this night

A photo of a ceiling stain lands in your texts. Then a voicemail from the plumber who “might” be free Thursday. Somewhere in email is last month’s quote you meant to compare. The sticky note on your desk still says Unit B — dishwasher?

None of it is dramatic on its own. Together it is a second job you never posted: chasing the story of every repair across three apps and your memory.

The work isn’t hard. Finding where you left it is.

The turn

What if the story lived in one place?

LandlordCrew is the maintenance crew that rides with you — not a property-management suite built for offices, but a phone app for landlords who still do the work themselves.

Tenants report without installing anything. Vendors answer with quotes and schedules. You see the request, the bid, and the job close — as one thread, not a scavenger hunt.

A week with the crew

Same leak. Different ending.

  1. Monday evening

    The request arrives where you look

    Your tenant opens the link you shared for their unit — no app download — snaps a photo, and sends it. You get a clear request: what’s wrong, how urgent, and the picture that proves it. No “can you resend that?”

  2. Tuesday lunch

    Vendors write into the same story

    You forward the job to the plumbers you trust. They open their link, submit a quote, propose a time. You compare apples to apples instead of reconstructing half-remembered phone calls.

  3. Thursday afternoon

    The job closes without a paper chase

    Work gets scheduled, status updates land where the request started, and completion is documented. When you need the history next year — for a dispute, a sale, or your own peace of mind — it’s still there.

The quiet

Your evenings get lighter

You still own the properties. You still choose the vendor. What changes is the noise: fewer “where did we leave that?” moments, fewer half-finished threads, fewer repairs that only live in your head.

LandlordCrew doesn’t replace judgment. It replaces the scramble so judgment has room to show up.

Less hunting. More closing.
